# THE BREW DOOR — Premium Website Design Brief

## Master brief

Design **The Brew Door** as a premium independent café brand, not as a generic restaurant website.

The website should feel tactile, atmospheric, editorial and slightly cinematic.

The visitor should feel like they have walked through the door of the café before physically visiting it.

Do not make it look like a SaaS landing page.

Do not make it look like a food-delivery website.

Do not use the generic “hero → cards → testimonials → footer” template.

The experience should have the visual confidence of an award-winning creative studio website, while retaining the usability of a high-converting local café website.

> **Important:** Use The Brew Door's actual Instagram, logo, menu, interiors, location, photography and brand assets as the source of truth. Do not invent business facts, menu items, prices, reviews, location or brand history.

# 2. Core visual idea

## “A digital doorway into the café.”

The name **The Brew Door** provides a strong interaction concept.

The website can metaphorically behave like a door.

## Opening experience

Full viewport.

Use a cinematic image or short video of real café material such as:

- Café entrance
- Coffee being poured
- Barista
- Steam
- Espresso extraction
- Hands preparing food
- Customers
- Close-up textures
- Interior atmosphere

Then reveal:

**THE BREW DOOR**

with extremely restrained typography.

Potential supporting copy, only if it fits the real brand:

> Come in. Stay awhile.

or

> Coffee, food & good reasons to stay.

Do not finalize copy until the real brand story is understood.

---

# 4. Hero animation

Do not use a generic loading spinner.

## Suggested opening sequence

1. Background starts dark.
2. Logo appears.
3. A thin vertical line expands.
4. Image reveals from behind the “door”.
5. Navigation fades in.
6. Page becomes interactive.

Target duration:

**~1.2–1.8 seconds maximum.**

Do not make users sit through a 5-second intro.

---

# 5. The “Door” micro-interaction

This could become the site's signature interaction.

When the visitor enters the experience, two visual panels can subtly slide apart like doors.

Do not make it look like a cheesy 3D door.

Keep it abstract and elegant.

Concept:

```text
       | THE BREW DOOR |
       |               |
       |     ENTER     |
       |               |

              ↓

       ←             →
```

The panels separate and reveal the café experience.

This creates a brand-specific interaction rather than borrowing a random trendy animation.

# 7. Typography

Use two complementary type personalities.

## Headings

Use a sophisticated editorial serif.

Potential references:

- Canela-like
- Cormorant Garamond
- Editorial New
- Instrument Serif
- DM Serif
- Carefully used Playfair Display

## Body/navigation

Use a clean grotesk/sans-serif.

Potential references:

- Inter
- Neue Montreal-like
- Helvetica Neue
- Satoshi
- Manrope
- Geist

## Principle

**Serif = emotion**

**Sans-serif = usability**

Example:

> **Coffee worth slowing down for.**

Large elegant serif.

Then:

> Espresso, brunch, pastries and everything in between.

Small clean sans-serif.

---

## Desktop interaction

Hovering a menu item can:

- Slightly enlarge the item

**Tiramisu**

# 12. Menu animation

Use subtle staggered reveals.

When the section enters the viewport:

- Item 1 appears
- Item 2 follows
- Item 3 follows
- etc.

Suggested stagger:

**100–150ms**

Avoid:

- Cards flying from every direction
- Rotating elements
- Bouncy animations
- Excessive 3D effects

The goal is **craft**, not “look how much animation we can add.”

---

# 13. Signature dish / signature drink

# 23. Scroll behavior

Use slow, controlled movement.

Recommended effects:

### Fade

`opacity: 0 → 1`

### Vertical reveal

`translateY(20px) → 0`

### Image clipping

Reveal imagery through a mask.

### Parallax

Extremely subtle:

**5–10% movement**

### Horizontal gallery

Use for one or two sections.

### Sticky storytelling

Image stays fixed while text changes.

This can work especially well for:

**The story of The Brew Door**

---

# 24. Do not overuse parallax

A common mistake is:

> “Premium = lots of movement.”

Wrong.

Premium is:

> **Controlled movement + excellent typography + excellent photography + whitespace.**

Animation should support atmosphere and hierarchy.

# 25. Cursor interactions

Desktop only.

Normal cursor:

`○`

Hovering an interactive object:

`EXPLORE`

or

`VIEW`

or

`→`

Possible behaviors:

- Photo → `VIEW`
- Menu → `TASTE`
- Instagram → `OPEN`

Keep the cursor UI tiny and refined.

# 26. Image hover

When hovering over a photograph:

- Image zooms approximately 1.03×
- Subtle grain can appear
- Caption can appear
- Cursor can change

Do not use an exaggerated 1.3× zoom.

# 27. Page transitions

Between pages/sections, use restrained transitions.

Potential language:

**dark → image → next page**

or

**cream → brown → cream**

A subtle full-screen color transition can make the website feel like one cohesive experience.

# 30. Design system

## Border radius

Avoid excessive rounded corners.

Prefer:

- 0px
- 2px
- 4px

for most elements.

Possibly use 12px for selected photographic modules.

The site should feel editorial rather than app-like.

---

# 31. Whitespace

Do not fill every pixel.

Use:

**Large margins + dramatic typography + photography.**

Think:

**Kinfolk-style editorial atmosphere**

rather than:

**food-delivery listing.**

---

# 32. Grid

Use a sophisticated editorial grid.

## Desktop

**12 columns**

## Mobile

**4 columns**

Photography can span:

- 4 columns
- 6 columns
- 8 columns
- 12 columns

Use varying spans to create rhythm.

Do not align everything into identical centered blocks.

Example:


# 34. Texture

A subtle grain overlay can add tactile quality.

Potential intensity:

**1–3% film grain**

Especially useful over large photographic sections.

Do not make the entire website look like a vintage Instagram filter.

# 41. Performance

This is non-negotiable.

> **Premium ≠ slow.**

Target:

- Fast initial render
- WebP/AVIF images
- Responsive image sizes
- Lazy-loaded below-the-fold media
- Compressed video
- Minimal JavaScript
- No unnecessary libraries
- CSS where possible
- Mobile-first performance

Do not add a heavy animation framework simply because it is popular.

---

# 42. Accessibility

Do not sacrifice usability for aesthetics.

Ensure:

- Good text contrast
- Readable body text
- Keyboard navigation
- Visible focus states
- Meaningful alt text
- Reduced-motion support
- Large enough mobile buttons
- Menu usable without hover
- No information conveyed only through animation

Implement `prefers-reduced-motion` and substantially reduce animation for users who request reduced motion.

---

# 43. Premium formula

A useful art-direction ratio:

### 40% — Photography

### 20% — Typography

### 15% — Layout

### 10% — Color / materiality

### 10% — Animation

### 5% — Micro-interactions

Most AI-generated websites reverse this and become:

- Too much animation
- Too many gradients
- Too many cards
- Too little actual brand

Avoid that.

# 45. Reddit / design-community lessons

Community feedback on café websites highlights several recurring mistakes.

## Common problems

- Too much whitespace without enough cohesion
- Narrow/tall image blocks
- Competing animation speeds
- Weak testimonial treatment
- Location/hours being difficult to find
- Menu being difficult to access
- Poor accessibility
- Generic service-business layouts
- PDF-only menus
- Too much experimentation at the expense of usability

## Key lesson

> **Do not add animation simply because you can.**

Every animation needs a reason.

# 47. Exact implementation direction for the agent

> **Create a premium, editorial café website for The Brew Door.**
>
> The visual language should combine boutique hospitality, specialty coffee culture, editorial magazine design and cinematic photography.
>
> The site should feel warm, sophisticated, tactile and human.
>
> Use The Brew Door's actual Instagram assets as the primary source of truth for its visual identity. Do not invent brand colors, interior details, menu items, prices, story or location.
>
> The design should be highly visual but restrained. Photography should dominate the emotional experience while typography creates hierarchy.
>
> Build a cinematic full-screen opening, subtle image transitions, editorial asymmetrical layouts, sophisticated serif/sans typography pairing, interactive menu exploration, atmospheric photography, subtle parallax and carefully controlled micro-interactions.
>
> The site's signature interaction can reference the name “The Brew Door” through subtle door-like reveals or transitions.
>
> Animation should communicate craft and atmosphere, not demonstrate technical ability.
>
> Prioritize mobile UX, performance, accessibility and conversion.
>
> A visitor must be able to discover the menu, opening hours, location and directions extremely quickly.
>
> The site should feel award-worthy on desktop while remaining extremely fast and practical on mobile.
>
> Think **Awwwards-level art direction + excellent local café UX**, rather than an experimental portfolio piece.
>
> Use real photography and real business information wherever available.
>
> Never fabricate information.

# 48. Final creative direction

If the entire brief has to be reduced to one sentence:

> ### “Make the website feel like a beautifully photographed evening inside The Brew Door, then turn that atmosphere into a ridiculously easy experience for finding the menu, deciding to visit, and getting there.”

The goal is not merely:

**Beautiful.**

And not merely:

**Functional.**

It should be:

## **Desirable + usable.**

The website should extend the physical café experience while making the practical decision to visit extremely easy.
